Parallel Prototypes

Sonic Focus is a browser based timer with immersive background sound. Its purpose is to support study or quiet work while allowing users to remain aware of a session without constantly checking the clock. These six parallel prototypes explore two areas: visualising time and experiencing time through sound. I have kept the interfaces simple so that users can focus on differences in experience, functionality rather than visual decoration.

Area 1 — Visualising Time

My first area of exploration concerns how remaining time is communicated visually. Despite a focus timer needs to make session progress understandable, a constantly visible countdown may also draw attention away from the activity. Therefore, I designed three different types of time visibility: an exact digital countdown, a circular representation of the remaining time, and a timer that stays hidden until requested. I want to see how difference in timer’s visual style can influence the experience.

Additionally, across these variations, I retain the same Start, Pause and Reset controls and the same session-length options. This keeps the basic interaction consistent while changing how users access time information. In this area, my main aim is to discover the relationship between usability, visual prominence and user control.

Prototype 1: Digital Countdown

This prototype was my initial design. It displays the timer in an explicit way, through showing the remaining minutes and seconds directly. I chose a classical numerical font to make the timer easy to read. The Start, Pause and Reset button sets directly below the countdown, linking with other control panels closely. It explores how exact information and simple layout can help users to better organise and operate the whole timer easily.

Prototype 2: Circular Progress Timer

This prototype variates from my initial design. I gained the inspiration from visual timer. It shows the remaining time through a shrinking circular ring, supported by a percentage label. Instead of emphasising individual seconds, the display shows the session as a whole and makes its remaining proportion visible. The circular form draws on the familiar appearance of a clock while changing the way of how user read the timer. I chose this mapping to explore whether an overview of progress can support time awareness during focused work.

Prototype 3: Hidden Timer

In this prototype, I tried a more interesting idea, which is hiding the countdown by default and provide a show time button. Users can reveal the remaining time when they want to check it, then hide it again. Basic status text remains visible so the interface can still communicate whether the session is ready, active, paused or complete. This choice shifts control over time visibility to the user. It explores a less visually persistent form of feedback while retaining access to precise information and functions, rather than removing them entirely.

Area 2 — Sound Experience

My second area explores how sound can communicate the passage of time when users are looking away from the screen. It considers how time can also provide session feedback, and interact with the sound experience. These three prototypes variates from use of start and end bells, additional intervel bells, and a background soundscape that gradually becomes quieter. In this area, I want to explore the balance between noticeable information and continuity of attention, rather than assuming that more frequent feedback is always desirable. Also, whether the whole experience still works without showing a visible timer.

Prototype 4: Start & End Bell

My first prototype uses Temple bell to mark the beginning and completion of a session, without a continuously visible countdown. The same sound identifies both boundaries, while the surrounding buttons and status text clarify whether the session is starting or ending. Resuming after a pause does not repeat the opening bell. I chose this limited use of sound to explore feedback at meaningful transitions, while leaving the middle of the session free.

Prototype 5: Halfway & Interval Bell

This prototype variate by adding a halfway bell and regular interval bells, while retaining Temple bell at the start and end. Halfway mode uses Temple bell once at the session midpoint; interval mode uses Singing bell every five minutes. In the 30-second test session, intervals occur every ten seconds. I chose these two patterns to explore occasional reassurance and repeated time awareness within the same prototype. Using a different sound for intervals distinguishes recurring progress cues from session boundaries without requiring users to watch the screen.

Prototype 6: Evolving Soundscape

For the final prototype, it maps remaining session time to the volume of the selected Rain or Forest soundscape. Starting the timer starts the background sound, which gradually decreases from 50% to 10% volume before stopping at completion. Pause and Reset also control the session audio. I chose continuous volume change to explore a gradual way of expressing time, compared with the separate events used by the bell prototypes. The change concerns loudness rather than a new melody or sound source; its perceptibility remains a question for peer testing.
